# Free Steam features worth knowing

Steam has been quietly building generous tools for adults for years, and
almost all of them are free if you already own any game. This chapter is
the guided tour — family comfort included — with the honest notes about
where each one helps and where it does not. (The
[glossary](glossary.md#steam-families) has the one-line version of
"Steam Families" that follows.)

## Steam Families — the household layer

Steam Families (Steam's 2024-and-later family system) lets up to a small
household of accounts share game libraries. How it is kind:

- **Shared library, less storage drama.** If the household owns a game,
  family members can play it without each buying a copy (one copy per
  concurrent session — when two people want the same game at the same
  hour, one buys; otherwise, sharing just works).
- **Parental settings in the same place.** Approval for purchases,
  content restrictions, and play-time limits — set once, for the kids'
  accounts, reviewed by the grown-up account holder.
- **Playtime reporting.** Grown-ups who want accountability (their
  own included) get sober, honest numbers.

Setup is under Settings → Family. It replaces the older "Family Sharing"
and "Family View" you may have heard of; old features roll into this one.

## Cloud saves, and their honest limits

Covered fully in [chapter 4](ch-04-save-backups.md#cloud-saves-are-a-convenience-not-a-backup);
the grown-up summary: turn it on per game (right-click game → Properties →
General → Steam Cloud), treat it as a convenience, and keep the Sunday
backup ritual anyway. When a cloud save misbehaves, your offline copy is
the one that saves you (see
[chapters 4](ch-04-save-backups.md) — really, the whole chapter).

## Screenshots, without a camera crew

Press **F12** in any game (Steam's default screenshot key) and a clean,
time-stamped PNG lands in your Steam screenshot library. From there you
can upload favorites to your profile — set ones you would like private.
The screenshot manager keeps them sorted by game and date, so in ten
years you will have a photo album of virtual flowers you actually
remembered to water (see [FFXIV's glamour](glossary.md#glamour) for the
fashion angle).

## Refunds, used without guilt

Steam's refund policy: return a game within **about two weeks** of
purchase if you have **under about two hours** of playtime. That is
enough to honestly answer "does this run on my machine, and is this game
actually for me?" — the grown-up question every game store should be
forced to ask. Using it once or twice a year is what the feature is
for; folks who do it habitually are the folks the store expects to buy
again after a kind experience.

The [glossary](glossary.md#refund) keeps the plain version of the rule.

## Wishlists and sale notices

Put a game in your wishlist and Steam tells you when it goes on sale
(and when it leaves early access, and similar). Grown-up pattern: add
games you are merely curious about, then wait for a real discount and a
quiet month. Half your future gaming angst will simply not happen.

## Remote Play Together — free multiplayer for couch games

Steam can "share" a couch game over the internet: one person owns the
game (or it is a free multiplayer title), everyone *else* does not, and
Steam handles it — each remote guest streams their window and can use
their own controllers. Wonderful for older friends' games and for
keeping a game like Overcooked or a card night alive across the miles.
See [chapter 8](ch-08-faraway-friends.md#remote-play-together) for the
friend-friendly context.

## Remote Play to your own devices

The same streaming idea pointed at yourself: play your Steam library on
a laptop in the other room, on the family TV, or (years later, when the
handhelds are good) on a portable device while the "real" machine does
the work. For grown-ups with one good machine and several rooms of a
home, this is the trick that makes the home feel bigger.

## Achievements, as a gentle scrapbook

Steam achievements are little trophies the game awards you. Nobody
cares about the count but you — and that's exactly why they work as a
digital memory of "the year I finally finished the fishing collection."

## A table to end the tour

| Feature | Where it is | What it saves you from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steam Families | Settings → Family | household storage fights; unsafe kid spending |
| Cloud saves | per-game Properties | progress loss across machines (with limits) |
| Screenshots | F12 button | blurry phone-across-the-room memories |
| Refunds | Help within two hours/weeks | buyer's remorse you cannot fix |
| Wishlists | the wishlist page | impulse-buy regret |
| Remote Play | friends list / Remote Play menu | pay-to-multiplayer on couch games |
