# FFXIV, step by step: from first install to keybind comfort

This is the companion chapter for Final Fantasy XIV, written like a
friend who has seen every beginner mistake would actually talk — warm,
specific, and arranged in the order you will hit each thing: trial,
purchase, download, first character, comfort settings, keybinds, and
backups of your own precious layout.

## Try before you buy

FFXIV has one of the most generous free trials in games, and it is the
grown-up starting point:

- It covers a huge slice of the game — the base story and several
  expansions' worth — with a level cap that has been raised over the
  years (it sat at **level 80** as of 2026; the official free-trial page
  has the current number).
- **No time limit** in the usual sense; it is not a weekend demo.
- Characters you make on the trial **carry over** if you later buy the
  full game — you never start over.
- Standouts you can do: the entire main story you can reach, dungeons
  with computer companions
  ([duty support](glossary.md#duty-support-and-trusts)), crafting,
  gathering, fishing, glamour dress-up, most of the world's exploration
  and stories.

Limits to know quietly (they're all in the official rules page): trial
accounts cannot join a [Free Company](glossary.md#free-company), cannot
use the [market board](glossary.md#market-board), cannot trade, cannot
hire [retainers](glossary.md#retainer), and chat is partly restricted.
Trial play is for trying; the full game opens the social economy.

## Choose your door (before anything else)

When you buy, you choose one storefront — and this choice sticks to your
account:

- **Steam version** — bought and launched through Steam; expansions
  bought through Steam.
- **Square Enix version (PC)** — bought from Web stores that sell the
  official launcher's registration codes; launched with the Square
  Enix [launcher](glossary.md#launcher).

The rule, in plain English: **the version you start with is the version
you keep buying.** A Steam-base game cannot take a Square Enix-bought
expansion and vice versa. Consoles are yet more separate licenses
(PC and console licenses do not cross, though a character can be
played on any platform you hold licenses for).

Two minutes here saves The Classic Sadness (buying the wrong expansion,
paying twice, [refunds](ch-05-steam-freebies.md#refunds-used-without-guilt)
giving you half your evening back):

1. Log into the launcher once with a Square Enix account before buying
   anything (make one free; it costs nothing).
2. Look at Mog Station ([glossary](glossary.md#mog-station)) and see
   what your account is marked as (Steam PC or Windows/other PC).
3. Then and only then buy expansions *matching* that label, from the
   matching store.

## Install reality

- The game (with expansions installed) is a *very large* download —
  comfortably more than a movie. On slow home internet, plan an
  overnight or a long afternoon; let it work while you do anything
  else.
- Install on an SSD if you have one — it won't change graphics, but it
  makes everything *open* faster (see
  [SSD advice](ch-02-hardware.md#reading-a-spec-sheet-in-plain-english)
  from chapter 2).
- Run the [official benchmark](ch-02-hardware.md#the-grown-up-buying-rule)
  first if you are unsure whether your machine will like the game. It
  is free, it does not require owning the game, and its grade is the
  honest answer.

## Pick your data center and world

Two levels of "where does my character live":

- **Data center** ([glossary](glossary.md#data-center)) — chosen by
  region and by friends. Different regions host different clusters;
  yours should match whoever you plan to play with most (see
  [chapter 8](ch-08-faraway-friends.md#ffxiv-specifics-staying-in-each-others-worlds)).
- **World** ([glossary](glossary.md#world)) — the individual server
  you call home. Any world on your data center works; friends on the
  same world is simplest.

You can visit other worlds freely later; choose lightly and change
your mind cheaply (a paid item moves your character's world — decide
once, smile twice).

## The shortest start

For a new character, in order:

1. **Pick a race and appearance you like** — there is no gameplay-
    correctness here; glamour and fights coexist forever (see
    [glamour](glossary.md#glamour)).
2. **Pick a starting class.** Do not agonize: your character can learn
    *every* job later (see [jobs](glossary.md#jobs-and-classes)), and
    the classic starter advice is "pick the one whose description makes
    you smile."
3. **Follow the main story quest** — the MSQ is the game's spine and
    unlocks nearly everything meaningful (see
    [main story quest](glossary.md#main-story-quest)). If you feel
    lost, "just do MSQ plus whatever looks fun near you" *is* the
    answer.
4. **Duty Finder does the group matching**; you never need to ask a
    stranger for a dungeon. Queues teach the game gently. Prefer a
    group of friendly robots? Story dungeons offer duty support, so
    even the shy can finish every story dungeon (see
    [glossary: duty support](glossary.md#duty-support-and-trusts)).
5. **Read zero wikis.** The sprout icon next to a new player's name
    (see [sprout](glossary.md#sprout)) tells the world "new player,
    here" and the community — including the Novice Network chat and
    its mentor volunteers (see
    [Novice Network](glossary.md#novice-network)) — is famously
    patient with it. You are allowed to simply ask.

## Comfort hour: settings a grown-up loves

Sometime in your first week, spend one honest hour in the menus. These
are the grown-up favorites:

- **Text sizes up.** Character Configuration → HUD and dialogue
  options — enlarge chat, quest text, anything you lean toward the
  screen to read. (See chapter 7 for why this is the first comfort.)
- **HUD layout.** The HUD arrangement screen lets you move every bar
  and box; move the parts you stare at to natural screen corners
  (top of the screen is eye-level heaven; see
  [chapter 7](ch-07-screens-and-eyes.md#set-the-screen-to-match-the-room)).
- **Loot rules and loot filters.** Tell the game what items matter to
  you so chat stays readable.
- **Glamour wardrobe early.** It's optional vanity and worth it; dress
  the character you enjoy looking at (see
  [glamour](glossary.md#glamour)).
- **Accessibility options.** Colorblind settings, reduced flashes, and
  targeting aids live in the menus and are worth a scan no matter who
  you are.

## Keybind comfort

Alright, the part the chapter title promised. The goal is not "optimal
rotations"; the goal is *no hand should ever travel far for a thing it
does often*. Three principles:

- **Reach beats beauty.** The keys your whole hand can hit without
  moving the wrist (1–5, Q, E, R, F, Tab, Shift/Control pairs) are the
  comfortable real estate. Put your most-pressed actions there.
- **Muscle memory loves consistent layout.** Same actions in the same
  [hotbar](glossary.md#hotbar) corners across jobs, so switching jobs
  means no re-learning.
- **Make it yours on purpose.** Defaults are fine starting points;
  remapping is a feature, not a confession (see
  [chapter 3](ch-03-controller-vs-keyboard.md#keyboard-and-mouse-the-short-version)).

Concretely, a kind starting layout for keyboard players:

- Move with `W A S D`; targeting walks through enemies with `Tab`
  (and back with a modified key).
- Action bars: row 1 = `1 2 3 4 5`; row 2 = same with `Shift` held;
  row 3 = same with `Control` held. `Q`, `E`, `R`, `F` for the actions
  you absolutely must find mid-fight (a gap-close, a self-heal, a
  stun).
- Text commands and macros (see below) get the far corners.

For controller players, the cross hotbar is the whole game plan:

- Actions live on a pad of buttons and shoulder-triggers — the arms of
  a cross. Two cross hotbars live at once: one visible, one *held open*
  by pressing a modifier, so 16+ actions are reachable without
  moving fingers off comfort.
- In Character Configuration → Controller, you can tune the hold
  buttons, the double-tap arms, and how targeting works (d-pad
  targeting with shoulder switching).
- Many veteran raiders play honest endgame difficulty on controller.
  If your hands already argue with mouse precision, controller in
  FFXIV is a first-class choice (see chapter 3's
  [genre table](ch-03-controller-vs-keyboard.md#the-genre-table-honest-version)).

## Macro kindness

FFXIV [macros](glossary.md#macro) are small saved lines you press to
run: a greeting when you join a party, a training-mode reminder, a
crafting loop. The kindness rules:

- Greeting macros should take one line of chat space — warm, not
  theatrical.
- Never macro spam into public channels or use macros to flood a
  chat.
- Macros are client-side data — see the backups below, or
  [chapter 4](ch-04-save-backups.md) for the general save-backup
  method.

## Backing up your character's comfort

Because FFXIV's *your-side* settings (keybinds, HUD, macros) are worth
more than the install itself, do these once this month (from
[chapter 4](ch-04-save-backups.md#the-launcher-backup-tool)):

1. **Launcher Backup Tool** — in the Square Enix launcher's config
   area, run Backup; it writes a single `FFXIVconfig.fea` file. Copy
   that file into your
   [Game Saves](ch-04-save-backups.md#the-grown-up-backup-method)
   folder, dated.
2. **Server-side character settings** — at the character-select gear
   icon, use Character Settings / Client Settings backup to store key
   settings on Square Enix's servers. It allows restore onto other
   platforms and after a clean reinstall. Upload once, download
   anywhere (there is a short per-day limit on uploads; that is normal).

With those two things done, a brand-new machine costs you login time —
not a night of retuning every bar by memory.

## A calm final section: the game is not in a hurry

The most soothing fact about FFXIV for grown-ups: **there is no daily
obligation**. Log in monthly? Fine. Binge a story then stop a season?
Fine. Pause the [subscription](glossary.md#subscription) for a few
months and come back? The game keeps your gear, your house (with an
understanding grace period, stopped from demolition if you are away),
and your friends list. Pause it; visit family; return when the sea
calls.

Sprout long, log off at reasonable hours, and welcome to Eorzea.
