# Screens, eyes, and kind breaks

You will never upgrade your eyes. Everything else in this manual is
about having fun for years; this chapter is about still enjoying that
fun with eyes that do not ache when you log off. None of this is medical
advice — it is comfort advice in the spirit of
[chapter 2](ch-02-hardware.md) (your body is a long-term investment).
If your eyes hurt persistently, a professional deserves the visit, not a
settings menu.

## The 20-20-20 rule, kept simply

Every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 meters) away, for
about 20 seconds. That is it — the whole famous rule. Why it works: when
you focus on a near screen, the focusing muscles in your eyes hold one
position; the break lets them relax. You do not need an app for this; a
wall clock or a window does the job.

Making it real takes a timer:

- Phone alarm or a cheap kitchen timer on the desk (kitchen timers are
  under-loved gaming equipment).
- Built-in OS nudges: Windows has break-reminder aids (Clock app →
  Focus sessions); macOS has Screen Time options; phones are excellent
  at reminding you about things, use them for your eyes too.

## Set the screen to match the room

Eye strain is mostly *contrast strain* — a bright screen in a dark room
(or the reverse) makes eyes adjust constantly, like reading by flashlight.

- **Match brightness to surroundings.** Screen roughly as bright as the
  paper next to it. Dim it at night alongside the lights; most additive
  eye comfort comes from "not glowing like a lighthouse."
- **Night color modes.** Windows (Night light), macOS (Night Shift),
  and many monitors warm the screen colors in the evening. Whether the
  science is done or not, most grown-ups find it pleasant — that is
  reason enough. Dark game modes are fine too, if the text stays
  readable.
- **Text size up.** In FFXIV and every launcher, HUD and text scale
  options exist — see [chapter 9](ch-09-ffxiv.md) for the HUD comfort
  hour. If you lean toward the screen to read chat, make the text
  bigger. Squinting is not a personality trait.
- **Distance and height.** Arm's length-ish; the top of the screen at
  or slightly below eye height. This is the part your neck will thank
  you for — see
  [chapter 3](ch-03-controller-vs-keyboard.md#first-the-hands) for the
  rest of the posture stack.

## Blinking, dryness, and honest hydration

Screens slow your blink rate; eyes dry out (contact lens wearers know
this intimately). The comforts: drink water (a giant glass within arm's
reach makes it inevitable), blink on purpose at the
[20-20-20](#the-20-20-20-rule-kept-simply) breaks, and artificial-tear
drops are a reasonable over-the-counter kindness if dryness is
persistent — again, a pharmacist can tell you the difference between
comfort and a symptom.

## Break points that games give you for free

Long online games like FFXIV hand you natural pauses — use them:

- **Queue time.** Matchmaking queues and dungeon queues are
  pre-built breaks. Stand up. Shake out hands. Look out a window.
- **Between duties.** After a dungeon run or a story chapter ends, log
  off *or* stretch. Logging off at a quest turn-in ("I'll just finish
  this quest line") is the healthiest stopping ritual ever invented.
- **Cutscenes** are an honest place to sip water, not to plow through
  with noodles balanced on the desk.

> The phrase "one more duty" has stopped more good evenings than lag
> ever did. Try "one more *stretch*, then one more duty."

## End-of-session check, in 15 seconds

Before you log off, a quick body scan as a friend would do it:

1. Are the eyes dry or sandy? — water, gentle blink break, lights up
   when you're done.
2. Neck and shoulders? — roll them once; tomorrow's you says thanks.
3. Hands? — see
   [chapter 3](ch-03-controller-vs-keyboard.md#first-the-hands) for
   wrist care; shake them out once.
4. Legs? — a real walk to the kettle, the window, another room. Two
   minutes.
5. Saves backed up?
   [Chapter 4](ch-04-save-backups.md#the-grown-up-backup-method) says
   Sunday; tonight is fine too.

And one positively grown-up ending: **say goodnight in chat and mean
it.** Friends who log off when they promised sleep are friends who are
still there in six months. See
[chapter 8](ch-08-faraway-friends.md) for the goodbye etiquette and
[chapter 9](ch-09-ffxiv.md) for FFXIV's own comfort settings — HUD
scaling included.
