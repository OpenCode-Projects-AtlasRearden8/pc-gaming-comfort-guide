# Choosing modest hardware, sensibly

Here is the grown-up secret nobody at the electronics store will tell you:
**most games worth playing run fine on modest hardware.** FFXIV in
particular is famously kind to older machines — it is an online game with
management-level rendering, and huge numbers of people play it daily on
6-year-old laptops. You do not need a machine that impresses strangers on
the internet. You need one that plays *your* games comfortably.

## The grown-up buying rule

Buy for the games you actually want to play, not for the machine you wish
you were. That is the whole rule; everything else is arithmetic.

Concretely: before you spend anything, run the game's own recommendation
or — much better — its **official benchmark** if it offers one. FFXIV has
a free official benchmark tool (search "FFXIV official benchmark") that
installs without needing the game, plays a small cutscene, and prints you
a grade. A passing grade from the benchmark is better information than any
spec sheet debate.

## Reading a spec sheet, in plain English

Spec sheets are written for spec writers. Here is the human translation:

- **CPU (processor)** — the brain. Handles logic, AI decisions, and
  overhead. For comfortable gaming, a modern mid-range CPU is plenty; you
  only need big brain-power for simulation-heavy games.
- **GPU (graphics card)** — the artist. Draws the world. This is the part
  your eye actually experiences. Our "modest" advice lives here: pick the
  cheapest option the game's official recommendation lists and *only*
  spend upward if the benchmark says so.
- **RAM (memory)** — the desk space. 8 GB is workable; 16 GB is
  comfortable and is the sweet spot for grown-ups who also
  browser-tabs. Beyond that, only heavy workloads care.
- **Storage (SSD vs older hard drive)** — the filing cabinet. An SSD
  (solid state drive) makes *everything* feel faster: boot, load screens,
  patch installs, [save-files](ch-04-save-backups.md#what-a-save-file-is).
  If your machine still runs on a spinning hard drive, upgrading this
  single part is the best-priced joy in all of gaming.
- **Monitor** — your actual view. A plain 1080p monitor at 60 Hz is
  perfectly pleasant for story games, FFXIV, and nearly anything grown-up
  about modernity. Bigger numbers help fast competitive games, which is
  not our lifestyle.

> If somebody sniffs at your setup: healthy save files, comfortable
> wrists, and paid-off bills beat frame counts every single time.

## Upgrade order, if you upgrade at all

When a game stutters or loads feeling dusty, upgrade in this order and
stop when it feels good again. This order works because it is cheapest
first and cheapest fixes the most common problems:

1. **SSD** — if you do not have one, this is non-negotiable joy.
2. **RAM to 16 GB** — cheap, easy, and it stops the annoying freezes.
3. **A [new launcher](ch-01-launchers.md) fresh start** — often the
   *real* fix; a clean install clears decades of weird files.
4. **GPU** — only if the benchmark says it is actually the bottleneck.
   Do not upgrade GPU for a game your eye cannot tell the difference on.
5. **Everything else** — only for a specific blocking reason.

## Laptop, desktop, or handheld?

- **Desktop**: easiest to upgrade, most upgrade room, most comfort
  (real keyboard positioning, proper screens at eye height). Best if you
  have a desk corner it can live in.
- **Laptop**: trades upgrade room for living wherever you do. Fine for
  story games and FFXIV, and a lap [desk](ch-03-controller-vs-keyboard.md#lap-and-couch-kindness)
  keeps it off your thighs. Get 16 GB and an SSD; check reviews for fan
  noise, since that is the grown-up complaint nobody mentions.
- **Handheld PC** (Steam Deck and its cousins): the couch option.
  Wonderful for story games, and FFXIV runs on them well. Note honestly
  the limits: patches are big; storage fills fast; input is the small
  [controller](ch-03-controller-vs-keyboard.md#first-the-hands) built in
  and is entirely workable.

## Where grown-up money multiplies (not hardware, mostly)

A short honest detour, because it is the real chapter of a grown-up guide:

- **A proper mouse** (or a controller that fits) beats a fancy GPU for
  daily feel.
- **A secondhand cushion for your chair** — cheap, transformational.
  Back comfort compounds. Chapter 3 and [chapter 7](ch-07-screens-and-eyes.md)
  treat your body as the long-term investment it is.
- **A sound square for the ears** — warm headphones you already like
  beat shiny new ones you must sell.
- **Time saved on patch management** — a 25-minute [launcher
  checklist](ch-01-launchers.md#the-launcher-setup-checklist) once per
  launcher setup is worth five gadgets in daily peace.

## The two-minute decision list

When you are looking at a machine (or a part), answer these four
questions, in this order:

1. **What do I actually play, or plan to play, named and specific?**
   ("FFXIV and a couple of story games" is a real answer. "Whatever"
   is not; that is how people buy servers.)
2. **Is there an official benchmark?** If yes, run it before spending
   anything, on the actual machine in question.
3. **Does it have an SSD and enough RAM that I won't fight the machine
   itself?** (16 GB and SSD are the grown-up floors.)
4. **Can I pay without guilt?** A machine that bought peace of mind and
   did not eat rent is eligible. If it will take six months to pay off,
   put it back on the shelf — better gaming later still beats nervous
   gaming now.
