# Controller or keyboard? A kindness guide

Somebody at some point will tell you one of these is "better." They are
not being mean; they are just wrong in a specific direction, for their own
hands. The real answer depends on three things: **the game's genre, your
physical comfort, and what your hands already know.** This chapter walks
each one.

## First, the hands

Neither input is "better" at being healthy. Both are fine if set up
kindly, and both will hurt you as slowly as you let them if you ignore
setup. The comfort rules that matter:

- **Neutral wrists.** Wrists straight, not bent up or sideways, whether
  holding a controller or a mouse. Elbows near your body, roughly a
  right angle. Not military posture — just not *folded like a pretzel*.
- **Shoulders down.** Controller players curl forward; mouse players
  climb. Catch yourself once a session and drop the shoulders.
- **Breathing breaks.** See [chapter 7](ch-07-screens-and-eyes.md) for
  the full session ritual; take hands out of grip every so often and
  shake them out.
- **Real pain is a stop sign.** Not a badge. Tingling, numbness, or
  persistent ache means change the setup, change the way you play, or
  take real days off. (This guide is comfort advice, not medical advice;
  a persistent problem deserves a professional, not a forum thread.)

A grown-up trick: whichever input you use, try the other one sometimes.
Different muscles, different fatigue. Variety is the wrist's best friend.

## The genre table (honest version)

| Game type | What feels kind | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story, adventure | Either, whatever's in reach | Precision rarely matters |
| FFXIV (MMO) | Both, officially supported | See below for the both-at-once trick |
| Platformers, racing, couch | Controller | Thumbsticks and triggers shine |
| Strategy, building, city | Keyboard + mouse | Pointer precision is the whole game |
| Shooters | Keyboard + mouse (or controller, kindly) | Pointer speed favors mouse; comfort may favor pad |
| Chess, puzzles | Whatever | Switch input mid-puzzle if you want |

## Keyboard and mouse, the short version

The mouse is a pointing stick; the keyboard is about memory. Two setup
moves make everything friendlier:

- **Anchor the mouse gently.** Relaxed grip; pad or desk edge under the
  wrist if it helps; keep it *stable* rather than moving all day.
- **Learn the movement keys by feel.** `W A S D` is the standard
  movement cluster; feel for the little bump on `F` (most keyboards have
  one) and your hand will find home without looking. Remap anything that
  makes you reach; [keybinds](glossary.md#keybind) are yours.

## Controller, the short version

Thumbsticks, triggers, a d-pad, more buttons than it looks like. Setup
moves that matter:

- **Fit first.** Controllers come in sizes; a heavier or smaller pad can
  fix years of "my hands cramp" overnight.
- **Thumbstick tension** — loose sticks are fine resting; a firm pad is
  fine too. Pick what your thumbs like over a long session, not a demo.
- **Vibration off if it bothers** — it's an option, not a personality.

## The FFXIV answer, which is genuinely both

Final Fantasy XIV supports controller and keyboard on PC *at the same
time*, and lets you swap between them mid-session — with no penalty and
no settings conflict. In practice:

- Many players use the **controller for combat** (cross hotbars organized
  and forgiving) and the **keyboard for chat**. It works together and it
  is entirely normal.
- The **cross hotbar** (see [chapter 9](ch-09-ffxiv.md#keybind-comfort))
  is the controller-specific control scheme; it keeps everything reachable
  and organized enough that raiders play high-difficulty content on pad —
  no one sensible argues about it.
- For targeting many small things quickly (the "MMO moments"), some
  keyboard-and-mouse motions can *still* help; you do not have to choose.

> The right answer is the one your hands stop complaining about. Try
> both, rotate between them on long nights, and take the ergonomic win.

## Lap and couch kindness

If you game on the couch (great), build the setup like a desk away from
desk:

- Keep the machine and drinks off the cushion (spills are a save file's
  worst enemy — see [chapter 4](ch-04-save-backups.md)).
- A small lap desk or pillow under a laptop keeps heat off your legs and
  vents clear.
- Controller cable or full battery before a long session; nothing ends
  a cozy session faster than a dying pad.
