# Glossary

Welcome to the part of the manual dedicated to explaining every word a
gamer friend has ever said to you with total confidence and no definition.
Every entry below is written the way you would say it at a kitchen table —
no assumed reflexes, no assumed gear, and no requirement to have ever
touched a joystick.

## 2FA

Two-factor authentication. When you log in, you supply something *you know*
(a password) and something *you have* (a code from your phone, an
authentication app, or a little hardware key). It turns a stolen password
into a dead key. Turn it on everywhere it is offered — game launchers
included. See [chapter 1](ch-01-launchers.md).

## Client

The program on your computer that actually runs the game. The launcher
invites it; the client *is* the game. When a patch misbehaves, it is the
client being updated.

## Cloud save

A copy of your save file that the game or launcher quietly uploads to its
servers. A convenience, not a backup plan — see
[save file](#save-file) and [chapter 4](ch-04-save-backups.md) for why you
want both.

## Co-op

Multiplayer where everyone works *together* toward a shared goal — building,
farming, dungeon-running — instead of against each other. The grown-up
comfort zone.

## Crossplay

Playing on a different platform than your friends — PC together with
console, for example — while still joining the same world or lobby.

## Data center

The regional cluster of servers an online game runs on. Everyone on the
same data center can see each other; different ones might as well be
separate planets. Choose yours for location and friends — in FFXIV it is
the biggest geographic decision you make. See
[world](#world) and [chapter 8](ch-08-faraway-friends.md).

## DLC

"Downloadable content." Extra paid content for a game you already own.
Nothing shady about the idea — it is simply ordering more of a meal you
liked.

## Duty

FFXIV's word for a mission-shaped activity: dungeons, trials, and raids.
You get matched into them through the Duty Finder. See
[duty roulette](#duty-roulette) and
[duty support](#duty-support-and-trusts).

## Duty Support and Trusts

Two of FFXIV's greatest kindnesses. Duty Support lets you run the *story*
dungeons with computer-controlled companions, so you never have to meet a
stranger to finish the plot. Trusts are the leveling-system cousin of the
same idea. Shy players rejoice.

## Duty roulette

FFXIV's matchmaking mode where you sign up for "a dungeon in this category"
and get matched with a party. Short queues, good experience points, and a
pleasant lottery of tonight's scenery.

## Free Company

FFXIV's name for a guild: a player club that shares a chat channel, buys
land, and makes the group feel a bit like a neighborhood. Trial players
cannot join one — see [subscription](#subscription) for when that opens up.

## FPS

Frames per second — how many complete images your computer draws each
second. A steady 30 is playable; a steady 60 is pleasant; a number you
reached by sacrificing comfort is not worth it. See
[chapter 2](ch-02-hardware.md) on picking modest hardware.

## Jobs and classes

The character type you play as. A class is what you start as; a job is
what it grows into or specializes as. In FFXIV one character does not
pick a job forever — every character can learn every job; you just
equip a different job stone to switch. Tank tonight, dancer tomorrow.

## Glamour

FFXIV's official dress-up system: it applies the *look* of one piece of
gear to another, so your character can be powerful and
respectably dressed at the same time. The true endgame is fashion.

## Guild

What most games call a persistent player club — FFXIV calls it a
[free company](#free-company).

## Hotbar

A row of action icons on your screen that you trigger with keys or
controller buttons. Organizing your hotbars is the heart of comfortable
play. See [keybind](#keybind), plus [chapter 9](ch-09-ffxiv.md) for
sorted-out ideas.

## HUD

Heads-up display: everything the game superimposes on your view — health
bars, chat windows, timers, the minimap. FFXIV lets you rearrange every
piece of the HUD, which is worth an honest hour of tuning.

## Keybind

A key or button you have assigned to do something. Changing keybinds to
suit your actual hands is not cheating; it is ergonomics. See
[chapter 3](ch-03-controller-vs-keyboard.md) and
[chapter 9](ch-09-ffxiv.md).

## Launcher

The program that greets you before the game does — store, library,
updates, friends list, settings, all in one doorway. See
[chapter 1](ch-01-launchers.md) for the full tour and what a trustworthy
one needs.

## Macro

A short saved script that runs something for you — a line of text, a few
actions in a row. In FFXIV, macros are a gentle tool for greetings and
crafting loops. They are stored on your computer, so back them up (see
[chapter 4](ch-04-save-backups.md)).

## Main story quest

Usually shortened to "MSQ." The big storyline the entire game is built
around — a long spine of quests that unlocks nearly everything important.
Follow it and you cannot truly get lost.

## Market board

FFXIV's player-to-player marketplace — think in-game classified ads, staffed
by moogles. Trial players can browse but not post.

## Microtransaction

A small in-game purchase — a costume, a convenience, a little pile of
optional fun. Treat them like snacks, not groceries, and budgets stay
intact. Big-picture spending sense lives in
[chapter 2](ch-02-hardware.md).

## Mod

A modification players add to a game — changing its look, difficulty, text,
or behavior, within whatever the game's maker permits. See
[chapter 6](ch-06-mod-safety.md) for plain rules (and FFXIV's special
situation).

## Mod manager

A tool that installs, tracks, and — crucially — cleanly *uninstalls* mods.
Why we prefer managers to hand-editing folders is covered in
[chapter 6](ch-06-mod-safety.md).

## Mog Station

The Square Enix website for your FFXIV account — where subscriptions are
paid, licenses are registered, and account details and security (see
[2FA](#2fa)) are managed.

## Novice Network

FFXIV's beginner chat channel, staffed by volunteer mentors who answer
new-player questions. Free advice with a friendly vibe. See
[sprout](#sprout).

## Party Finder

FFXIV's recruitment board: build a group, or find one, for a specific duty.
It works across worlds, which is exactly how faraway friends pick each
other up. See [chapter 8](ch-08-faraway-friends.md).

## Patch

An official update to the game itself — fixes, new features, balance
changes. Patch day usually means a big download and a small wait.

## Ping and lag

Ping is how many milliseconds a round trip to the game server takes; lag
is the missed inputs, hitches, and slides that a big number causes. Lower
ping is better, a wired connection beats wireless, and patience beats
everything.

## Refund

Most stores (Steam famously) let you return a game you did not sink much
time into: under about two hours of play and within about two weeks of
purchase. Use it without guilt when a game simply does not fit. See
[chapter 5](ch-05-steam-freebies.md).

## Retainer

FFXIV: your helpful employee — a small NPC that holds extra inventory and
sells items for you while you are offline. Trial players cannot hire them;
full players adore them.

## Save file

The file that remembers exactly how far you got in a game. Where it lives
varies by game; where it *should* live is "in at least two places." Read
[chapter 4](ch-04-save-backups.md), then thank future-you.

## Sprout

The little green-plant icon beside the names of brand-new FFXIV players.
Wear it proudly — everyone worth playing with used to be one.

## Steam Families

Steam's free household grouping: share game libraries with family, set
parental controls, and see playtime reports in one place. Tour available in
[chapter 5](ch-05-steam-freebies.md).

## Subscription

A recurring payment that keeps an online game open to you — FFXIV works
this way. You may pause a subscription and come back later; the game will
not be offended, and your gear will all still be there.

## Tank, healer, DPS

The three combat roles in group play. The **tank** walks in front and takes
the hits; the **healer** keeps everyone standing; the **DPS** does the
damage. A friendly group will happily help a first-timer — and with
[duty support](#duty-support-and-trusts) you can practice with no
strangers at all.

## World

An individual server inside a [data center](#data-center). In FFXIV your
character has a home world; you can visit others, but your friends list,
housing, and club life are anchored at home. See
[chapter 8](ch-08-faraway-friends.md).
