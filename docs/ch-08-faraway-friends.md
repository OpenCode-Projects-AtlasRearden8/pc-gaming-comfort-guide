# Playing with faraway friends

Grown-ups play games with people they love who are far away — a sibling
two time zones off, an old roommate, a friend who moved for work, a
family spread across a map. Online play is a way of sitting in the same
room at a distance. This chapter is about doing it kindly: the voice
parts, the scheduling parts, and the FFXIV-specific parts
([data centers](glossary.md#data-center) matter *so much* here).

## Voice chat: the quiet basics

Talking while playing is 80% of "being in the same room." The setup:

- **A headset beats speakers.** Echo disappears; the friend's game
  sounds stop bleeding into the mic; everyone's patience doubles.
- **Discord, Steam voice, or in-game chat** — all work; pick one for a
  friend group and stick with it. Voice chat in FFXIV's own party tools
  also exists and works fine for dungeon groups.
- **Learn push-to-talk.** A key held while you speak keeps the family
  study's door noise out of the party's ears. Open-mic is fine for
  close friends; push-to-talk is the considerate default with anyone
  new.
- **A 30-second mic check** before the first game together prevents an
  evening's confusion: "can you hear me? is there double audio?"

> Kindness note: your voice arriving during someone else's dungeon pull
> is the grown-up equivalent of shouting across a library.

## Scheduling across time zones, like adults

Faraway play dies from vague "sometime?" plans. The remedies are
unheroic and effective:

- **Name a day and a time — in both time zones.** "Tuesday 8 p.m. your
  time / 11 p.m. mine" prevents an entire genre of sadness. Calendar
  apps and phone alarms settle it further.
- **Standing monthly-ish times are friendlier than perfect daily
  punctuality.** One reliable evening every week or two beats
  ambitious daily plans that quietly rot.
- **Half-full plans are allowed.** "I can do an hour from 7 to 8" is a
  complete invitation, not an apology. Grown-up play is stoppable — see
  [chapter 7](ch-07-screens-and-eyes.md)'s honest goodbyes.

## Choosing games that fit grown-up sessions

Not every game stretches well across an ocean. The reliable shapes:

- **15–40 minute co-op games** (dungeon-type games, tower-defense
  nights, the cooking-chaos genre). If one of you has a crisis at
  7:45, no one is stranded.
- **Turn-based games** — chess, card games, 4X epics played over days.
  The time zone difference becomes a feature.
- **FFXIV's regular duties** are exactly the right length — 15–30
  minute group missions you queue together through the
  [Duty Finder](ch-09-ffxiv.md#the-shortest-start). Long raids and
  trial runs need a committee and a calendar; start with the short
  ones.

## FFXIV specifics: staying in each other's worlds

This is the part new FFXIV players learn *after* the mistake — learn it
now:

1. **Data center first, friends second.** You each pick a data center
   when creating your first character (see
   [chapter 9](ch-09-ffxiv.md#pick-your-data-center-and-world)). North
   American, European, Japanese, and Oceanic data centers each host
   several [worlds](glossary.md#world). Friends on the *same* data
   center see each other routinely; friends on different data centers
   can visit — but group play and your shared weekly life go easiest
   on the same one. **Before creating characters, agree on the data
   center.** (Name one world too — it makes meeting up trivial.)
2. **World visiting is easy; living together is better.** You can
   visit ("World Visit") a friend's world to play and group, but your
   [Free Company](glossary.md#free-company), your housing neighbors,
   and your recruiting grounds live on your home world.
3. **Trial players have limited socials.** On the
   [free trial](ch-09-ffxiv.md#try-before-you-buy) you cannot join a
   Free Company and chat is restricted — so the trial is for trying
   the game, and a fair warning: budgets of friendship go gently there
   (see [subscription](glossary.md#subscription) for how the full game
   opens everything).
4. **Cross-world parties work through Party Finder.** The
   [Party Finder](glossary.md#party-finder) board can pull a group
   from other worlds on your data center; that is the standard way
   "world A" meets "world B" friends for a dungeon night.
5. **The Novice Network is a place strangers are kind.** Bring a
   faraway friend there to meet people locally (see
   [sprout](glossary.md#sprout) and
   [Novice Network](glossary.md#novice-network)).
6. **Free Companies are the long game.** A shared club with its house
   and its chat channel is the FFXIV answer to "we have somewhere we
   all belong." Worth planting early (it needs the full game).

## Remote Play Together

For couch-style games outside MMOs, Steam's
[Remote Play Together](ch-05-steam-freebies.md#remote-play-together-free-multiplayer-for-couch-games)
lets one owner's game be streamed to friends, each using their own
controllers, like sitting on the same couch from four countries. It
works with the household-friendliest games and costs nothing.

## An honest closing note

The most important part of faraway friendships in games is the goodbye:
log off when you said you would, don't keep a friend's tired hands
clicking for one more dungeon, and text the friend the next day about
something *other* than the game. You are not maintaining a gaming
schedule; you are keeping a friendship that happens to have a world in
it.

If FFXIV is calling: [chapter 9](ch-09-ffxiv.md) walks you — and a
faraway friend — from install to keybind comfort.
