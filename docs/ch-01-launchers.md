# Your game launcher, explained kindly

Before a game ever appears on your screen, something else appears first:
the **launcher**. It is the program that opens the front door — the game
store, your library of games, the update machinery, your friends list, and
your account settings, all living in one app. You will meet a new one every
few years, and it always feels like being handed a new kitchen appliance
with no instructions. This chapter is the instructions.

The [glossary](glossary.md#launcher) has the one-line definition; this
chapter has the grown-up tour.

## What a launcher actually does

Strip away the neon and a launcher does four jobs:

- **Sells and stores.** You buy games once; the launcher remembers you
  own them, forever, in a list called your *library*.
- **Updates.** Games are never finished anymore — patches arrive weekly
  or monthly. The launcher downloads and installs them while you go make
  tea. See [patch](glossary.md#patch) in the glossary.
- **Connects.** Friends list, voice chat, achievements, screenshots.
  Small features that quietly become important.
- **Protects.** Account security (see
  [2FA](glossary.md#2fa)), cloud saves (see
  [cloud save](glossary.md#cloud-save)), refund policies (see
  [refund](glossary.md#refund)), and parental settings.

A good launcher is a coat closet: boring, means well, holds everything.
A *bad* launcher is a coat closet that tries to be a nightclub.

## The launchers you will actually meet

- **Steam** — the big one for PC. Store, community, family controls,
  cloud saves, screenshots, the works. Chapter 5 is a guided tour of its
  free features.
- **Epic Games Store, GOG, Humble** — other storefronts. Each gives away
  free games occasionally and each is a perfectly reasonable place to buy.
- **Publisher launchers** — some makers run their own (Electronic Arts,
  Ubisoft, and famously Square Enix for FFXIV). These tend to be spartan:
  log in, patch, play.

## The FFXIV launcher, specifically

Final Fantasy XIV does not live inside Steam's bookshelves (well — there
*is* a Steam version, more on that in a moment). It ships with its own
Square Enix launcher, which is famously modest: a login box, a patch
button, news headlines, and a doorway into the game.

Three things worth knowing about it:

1. **Mog Station is the half the launcher isn't.** Your subscription,
   purchases, and account security live on the Mog Station website. When
   something says your account is "via Mog Station," that is where you
   manage it (see [Mog Station](glossary.md#mog-station)).
2. **The Steam-version trap.** You may buy FFXIV from Steam *or* from
   Square Enix directly — but each purchase stamps your account as a
   Steam-account or a Square Enix-account, and expansion purchases must
   match. Mixing them means buying licenses you cannot use. This is the
   single most expensive beginner mistake in the game, and it is entirely
   avoidable with two minutes of attention. See
   [chapter 9](ch-09-ffxiv.md#choose-your-door-before-anything-else) before your first
   purchase.
3. **It can back up your settings before you ask.** The launcher's config
   button opens a backup tool that exports your keybinds, macros, and HUD
   layout into a file you can keep anywhere. Chapters
   [4](ch-04-save-backups.md#what-the-stores-back-up-for-you) and
   [9](ch-09-ffxiv.md#keybind-comfort) go deeper.

## What makes a launcher trustworthy

Before you put real money in a launcher, run this five-point checklist.
Two minutes now saves weeks of annoyance later:

- **Account recovery works.** A recovery email you actually check; a
  password from a password manager, not from your memory (memory is a
  leaky vault).
- **2FA is on.** Authenticator app or a little physical key. See
  [glossary: 2FA](glossary.md#2fa).
- **The library syncs.** If the cloud-save option exists per game, know
  which of your games use it — and do not mistake it for a backup (that
  is [chapter 4's](ch-04-save-backups.md) job to correct).
- **Payment methods are minimal.** One card or wallet service. Avoid
  storing cards in four different launchers "just in case."
- **You know where the exit is.** Any launcher is used less than every
  month at least once; uninstalled is a valid option and your account
  survives it.

> Prefer accounts that support password managers and app-based 2FA
> over those that demand your phone number. Fewer places where your
> personal data lives is its own kind of comfort.

## The launcher setup checklist

If you have just installed a launcher, tonight's homework is fifteen
quiet minutes:

1. Account has a real name you are okay showing others; a recovery email
   that is not your bank's; and a strong password.
2. 2FA enabled, and the backup codes printed or written down somewhere
   that is not on the same device.
3. Family settings looked at once (kids? then
   [Steam Families](glossary.md#steam-families) in chapter 5 is your
   friend).
4. Screenshots folder located (Steam: F12 by default). You will use it
   more than you think — see [chapter 7](ch-07-screens-and-eyes.md) for
   eye-friendly sessions full of them.
5. One bookmarked store page for the game you care about, so you can
   check news instead of doomscrolling forums.

That's it. It's boring. It works.
